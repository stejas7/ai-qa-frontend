# Video 3 — Market View + How AI UAT Engineer Works

**Target length:** 3:30–4:30
**Format:** 16:9, 1080p
**Audience:** Engineering leaders, buyers, founders, enterprise QA/UAT teams
**Purpose:** Explain why agentic software is becoming a workforce category, where AI UAT Engineer fits, and how the product architecture and pricing model work.

## Evidence-backed market points
Use only sourced claims in the final edit.

- MarketsandMarkets reports the AI agents market at **$5.26B in 2024**, **$7.84B in 2025**, and forecasts **$52.62B by 2030**, a **46.3% CAGR from 2025–2030**.
- Microsoft’s 2025 Work Trend Index reported that **46% of leaders said their organization was already using agents to fully automate workstreams or business processes**.

Do not present modeled intermediate annual market values as measured market data. If a chart includes them, label them as interpolated planning points.

## Scene-by-scene storyboard

### Scene 1 — Market shift | 0:00–0:20
**Visual:** Pricing page market graph, then quick cuts of software delivery, QA dashboards and agent icons.
**On-screen text:** `AI agents are moving from assistants to digital labor`
**Voice-over:**
“AI agents are moving beyond chat and copilots into systems that can execute multi-step business processes. That shift matters for software delivery because UAT is still one of the most manual, coordination-heavy stages of release.”

### Scene 2 — Market data | 0:20–0:45
**Visual:** Animate market chart from 2024 to 2030.
**On-screen text:** `$5.26B → $52.62B • AI agents market`
**Voice-over:**
“MarketsandMarkets estimates the AI agents market at 5.26 billion dollars in 2024 and forecasts 52.62 billion dollars by 2030, with a 46.3 percent CAGR from 2025 to 2030.”

### Scene 3 — Adoption signal | 0:45–1:05
**Visual:** Hiring/adoption cards on Pricing page.
**On-screen text:** `46% already using agents to automate workstreams`
**Voice-over:**
“Microsoft’s 2025 Work Trend Index found that 46 percent of leaders said their organizations were already using agents to fully automate workstreams or business processes. The enterprise question is shifting from ‘Can AI help?’ to ‘Which work should agents own, and where must humans remain responsible?’”

### Scene 4 — Why UAT is a good agentic workflow | 1:05–1:35
**Visual:** Requirement document → test design → browser → evidence → release gate.
**On-screen text:** `Repeatable • evidence-heavy • policy-bound`
**Voice-over:**
“UAT is well suited to agentic automation because the workflow is repeatable, evidence-heavy and still needs governance. Requirements must be interpreted, tests selected, environments checked, browsers driven, evidence captured and release risk explained.”

### Scene 5 — Where AI UAT Engineer fits | 1:35–2:05
**Visual:** How It Works product flow and architecture cards.
**On-screen text:** `Specialist workforce for UAT`
**Voice-over:**
“AI UAT Engineer is designed as a specialist digital UAT workforce. It combines Spring AI and RAG for reasoning, a bounded catalog of specialist agents for planning and review, Playwright for browser execution, PostgreSQL and pgvector for application and retrieval data, and governed release controls for the final decision.”

### Scene 6 — Why 100 agents does not mean 100 always running | 2:05–2:30
**Visual:** 100-agent catalog → selected 5–10 agents.
**On-screen text:** `Catalog of 100 • bounded mission team`
**Voice-over:**
“The one-hundred-agent model is a skills catalog, not a promise to run one hundred agents on every mission. The optimizer selects a bounded specialist team based on requirement risk, business criticality, environment, test budget and release policy.”

### Scene 7 — Commercial model | 2:30–2:55
**Visual:** Pricing cards.
**On-screen text:** `$50 / agent-day • pooled monthly and annual plans`
**Voice-over:**
“The commercial model is based on agent-days so customers can buy capacity instead of unlimited compute. Daily capacity starts at fifty dollars per specialist agent-day, while monthly and annual plans pool committed usage and allow controlled burst capacity.”

### Scene 8 — How It Works page | 2:55–3:35
**Visual:** Scroll slowly through `/how-it-works`: product overview, product flow, architecture, technology, database/traceability and roadmap.
**Voice-over:**
“The How It Works page is the product’s visual map. It explains what the product does, how the architecture connects, which technologies are used, how data and traceability flow through the platform, and how the roadmap evolved from M1 through M100.”

### Scene 9 — M100 message | 3:35–3:58
**Visual:** M1–M100 roadmap ending at Enterprise Autonomy Readiness.
**On-screen text:** `M100 • Enterprise Autonomy Readiness`
**Voice-over:**
“The final roadmap phase is not uncontrolled autonomy. M100 combines governance coverage, model readiness, resilience validation, rollback readiness, audit completeness and human override analytics into an enterprise autonomy readiness state.”

### Scene 10 — Close | 3:58–4:15
**Visual:** Product landing page with How It Works and Pricing buttons.
**On-screen text:** `AI recommends • policy controls • humans retain authority`
**Voice-over:**
“The product thesis is straightforward: use digital labor to reduce repetitive UAT work, but keep decisions explainable, evidence-backed and governed.”
