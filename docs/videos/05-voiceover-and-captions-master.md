# AI UAT Engineer — Voiceover & Caption Master

Use this as the recording baseline. Speak naturally at roughly 135–150 words per minute. Product UI labels should be spoken exactly as deployed.

# Video 1 — Product Overview

## VO1 — Opening
“Software teams still spend significant time converting business requirements into test scenarios, coordinating UAT, collecting evidence, and deciding whether a release is ready. AI UAT Engineer brings those activities into one governed workflow.”

## VO2 — Connect the product
“Start by registering the product and environment you want to test. The product becomes the boundary for requirements, credentials, missions, execution and evidence.”

## VO3 — Start UAT
“Next, provide the requirement. AI UAT Engineer analyzes the requested behavior, uses available product context and retrieval, and prepares the UAT mission.”

## VO4 — AI workforce
“The platform contains a catalog of specialist AI agents. It does not blindly run one hundred agents for every task. The orchestrator selects a bounded specialist workforce based on the mission, risk, skills and execution budget.”

## VO5 — Execute
“Selected tests move into browser and API execution. The Execution Center shows total executions, passes, failures, duration and diagnostic information so a user can see what actually happened.”

## VO6 — Evidence
“Each important result is tied to evidence. Screenshots and execution details make the recommendation inspectable instead of asking a release team to trust an opaque AI answer.”

## VO7 — Release intelligence
“The platform combines requirement coverage, execution results, risk, policy and evidence into release intelligence. AI recommends; deterministic controls and authorized humans retain release authority.”

## VO8 — M1 to M100
“The roadmap now spans M1 through M100: platform foundations, enterprise controls, the agent workforce, adaptive UAT intelligence, portfolio orchestration, resilience and enterprise autonomy governance.”

## VO9 — Close
“AI UAT Engineer is designed to turn UAT from a manual coordination bottleneck into governed, evidence-backed software delivery capacity.”

# Video 2 — Step-by-Step Walkthrough

## VO1 — Login
“Open AI UAT Engineer and sign in with an authorized account or supported single sign-on provider. Public pages explain the product; operational work begins after authentication.”

## VO2 — Company and roles
“Every operational user belongs to an authorization boundary. Company-level administration manages its own users and products, while platform-level administration is reserved for platform operations.”

## VO3 — Register product
“Register the application you want the AI workforce to test. Give it a clear name and target environment. This keeps requirements, missions and evidence associated with the correct product.”

## VO4 — Credentials
“If the target application requires authentication, configure credentials through the product’s controlled credential workflow. Secrets should never be pasted into requirements, screenshots or documentation.”

## VO5 — Start UAT
“Choose the product and provide the business requirement. The requirement is the mission input: what behavior should exist, what outcome matters, and what the system needs to validate.”

## VO6 — Planning
“The platform analyzes the requirement, retrieves relevant context and selects specialist agents. Agent selection is bounded so the mission uses the skills it needs without creating uncontrolled cost or concurrency.”

## VO7 — Progress and statuses
“Follow the mission status as it moves through planning and execution. Read the exact status shown in the current UI. A queued state means work is waiting to begin; a running state means the mission is actively being processed; a completed state means processing has finished; and a failed or blocked state requires review.”

## VO8 — Results
“Open Results to understand what passed, what failed and what still needs attention. A result is useful only when it can be traced back to the requirement and supporting execution.”

## VO9 — Execution Center
“The Execution Center is the detailed operational view. It shows total execution, passed and failed steps, pass rate, latest execution, duration and diagnostic messages.”

## VO10 — Evidence
“For any important pass or failure, open its evidence. Evidence is especially useful for diagnosing a failure, confirming browser behavior and supporting release review.”

## VO11 — Release review
“Release intelligence combines these signals into a recommendation. The platform is designed for governed autonomy: AI can analyze and recommend, while policy and authorized humans control decisions that require approval.”

## VO12 — Rerun
“When a requirement or implementation changes, rerun the affected scope rather than treating every release as a completely new manual exercise. The goal is faster feedback with preserved traceability.”

## VO13 — Close
“That is the core AI UAT Engineer workflow: connect a product, provide the requirement, let the bounded AI workforce plan and execute, inspect evidence, and make a better-informed release decision.”

# Video 3 — Market + How It Works

## VO1 — Market
“AI agents are moving from conversational assistants toward systems that can perform multi-step work. Industry forecasts show rapid growth in the AI-agent market, while enterprise surveys show organizations already experimenting with agents for automated workstreams.”

## VO2 — Why UAT
“UAT is a strong fit for this model because it combines analysis, test design, execution, evidence collection, triage and release coordination. Those activities are repetitive, but they also need governance and traceability.”

## VO3 — Commercial model
“AI UAT Engineer prices capacity as an AI workforce. Daily usage supports agent-day consumption, while monthly and annual packages provide pooled capacity and burst access. This keeps the commercial model connected to actual autonomous work rather than pretending compute is unlimited.”

## VO4 — How it works
“The workflow starts with the product and requirement. Spring AI and retrieval provide context. The orchestrator selects a bounded set of specialist agents. Tests execute through browser and API automation, and evidence is persisted for inspection.”

## VO5 — Architecture
“The product uses a React front end, Spring Boot and Spring AI services, PostgreSQL with vector capabilities, Playwright execution, durable evidence, Docker delivery and AWS deployment.”

## VO6 — Governance
“The important design choice is governance. A larger agent catalog creates specialization, but each mission remains bounded. Policies, audit evidence, approval controls and human override keep autonomy accountable.”

## VO7 — Roadmap
“Across M1 to M100, the product evolves from UAT foundations into adaptive intelligence, cross-release memory, business journey coverage, workforce and portfolio planning, resilience, model governance and enterprise autonomy readiness.”

## VO8 — Close
“The market opportunity is not simply replacing a tester with a chatbot. It is building governed digital capacity that can execute UAT work, preserve evidence and help engineering teams release with greater confidence.”

# Caption rules
- Maximum two lines on screen.
- Prefer 32–42 characters per line.
- Do not caption secrets, email addresses or customer identifiers that appear incidentally.
- Keep product terms consistent: `AI UAT Engineer`, `UAT`, `AI workforce`, `evidence`, `release intelligence`.
- When showing market statistics, put source and year in small text on the same scene or in the final source card.
